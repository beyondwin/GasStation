## Design Context

### Users
GasStation is a Korean Android app that helps drivers compare nearby gas stations by current location, price, distance, brand, fuel type, watchlist state, and external map handoff. The product baseline targets fast, trustworthy comparison under driving pressure.

### Brand Personality
The interface should feel trustworthy, fast, and urban. The strongest visual association is a city public information sign: high-contrast, direct, and easy to scan under time pressure. The deeper craft goal is precise and production-grade rather than decorative.

### Aesthetic Direction
The production Urban Signal system uses an ivory `#FFFCF2` canvas, `#222222` black chrome, and `#FFDC00` yellow decision signals. Price remains the first-read element: 32sp in Nearby and 28sp in Watchlist. Borderless station rows, flat settings rows, and disciplined dividers replace generic nested cards. Distance, brand, watch state, freshness, and failure states support the price decision without competing with it.

### Design Principles
1. Price is the hero, everything else is context.
2. Every screen should prove the same design system: tokens first, feature screens second.
3. Use bold contrast with disciplined rhythm, not visual noise.
4. Prefer fast scanning over ornamental polish.
5. Make production quality visible: clear state handling, consistent components, accessible interaction targets, and predictable typography roles.
6. Preserve real brand artwork: RTO/RTX/NHO map to `ic_rtx`, ETC maps to `ic_etc`, and SKE/GSC/HDO/SOL/E1G/SKG keep their checked-in drawables without recoloring or cropping.
7. Use `주변·관심·설정` as the persistent top-level navigation; hide it only on SettingsDetail.

## Maintenance Priority

The current UI implements the shared Urban Signal hierarchy. Future UI work should preserve it:

1. Check `core:designsystem` tokens and shared chrome components before adding feature-local UI patterns.
2. Keep borderless station-row hierarchy ordered around price, distance, station name, brand, watch state, and delta.
3. Keep status, empty, loading, permission, GPS, and stale-cache states inside the shared guidance system.
4. Keep Watchlist rows at 108–116dp in the 360dp × 800dp default viewport, with five complete rows visible; at 200% font scale rows must expand without clipping and remain scrollable.
5. Do not repeat visible brand labels next to Watchlist logos; accessibility copy and ASCII test tags remain separate contracts.
6. Use Roborazzi and device screenshots to confirm Nearby, Watchlist, Settings, shared states, and README/demo evidence still read well at glanceable speed.
